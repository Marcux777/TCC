\noindent\textbf{Relevance and Fit to \textit{Production}:}\\
During seasonal harvest peaks, agro-industrial grain terminals experience severe bottlenecking due to physical contention for shared infrastructure—most notably shared weighbridges used for both gross and tare weighing. While traditional literature often approaches yard management via rigid First-In, First-Out (FIFO) rules or complex offline mathematical programs, these methods fail to adapt when real-world disruptions strike (e.g., rainfall over open hoppers, dumper mechanical failures, or administrative document holds).

Our study addresses this critical gap through the lens of \textbf{Logistics 5.0 in Production Engineering}. By formulating the terminal as a Dynamic Reentrant Hybrid Flow Shop (RHFS), we design and evaluate an online constrained dispatching decision-support engine that combines strict feasibility filtering with local short-horizon lexicographic ranking and an auditable event ledger. Our engine decouples reentrant contention, yielding a 44.8\% reduction in peak queue waiting times (p95) under heavy congestion while fully preserving terminal throughput and guaranteeing zero operational constraint violations. Crucially, the system preserves human-centricity by providing explainable recommendations and supporting auditable supervisory interventions.
